workLead: >-
  Three problems of three different shapes, from one product surface: a
  reliability problem, a choice between mechanics, and a commercial one.

principles:
  - title: Start with the real problem, not the assigned one
    body: >-
      The brief and the bottleneck are often different things, and the gap
      between them is where most of the value is.
    example: >-
      My roadmap said engagement. The app took fifteen seconds to open. Features
      shipped onto that are only ever measured on the people patient enough to
      wait, so I spent eight weeks on launch time first.
    href: /work/step-syncing
  - title: Use evidence before instinct
    body: >-
      Instinct picks the option you already like. Evidence tells you which
      option the problem actually has.
    example: >-
      Cohort analysis put a drop-off on the table instead of a feature request.
      Three strategies were live options; I scored them on what each one costs
      after launch, and only one had no recurring bill.
    href: /work/steps-premier-league
  - title: Make the trade-off explicit
    body: >-
      A decision with no cost was not a decision. Naming what it costs at the
      time is judgment; naming it afterwards is a defence.
    example: >-
      Choosing competition meant accepting that a standings table is a daily
      notice to whoever is last. That is a real cost, and it belongs in the same
      sentence as the choice.
    href: /work/steps-premier-league
  - title: Measure what shipped, and say what it proves
    body: >-
      A number without its population is a claim. A number with one is evidence,
      and the limits are part of the finding.
    example: >-
      Session time moved 3.5 → 7.8 minutes. It is also a contestable north star
      in a health app, because a product that works can shorten a session. I'd
      rather argue that out loud than quietly pick a safer metric.
    href: /approach
