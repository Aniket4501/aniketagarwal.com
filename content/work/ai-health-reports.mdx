## On paper this was a personalisation project

HCL Healthcare's consumer health app served more than a million registered users. I built the AI
Health Reports on it 0→1: the product requirements, the UX flow, and the personalisation logic that
decides what an individual report says about the person reading it.

Read that scope back and it is a consumer brief. Build it well and the person who opens the report
gets something worth opening. The framing is not wrong. It is incomplete, and the incompleteness is
where the money was.

**The beneficiary reads the report, the employer buys the programme, and the report is the thing that
makes the programme legible at renewal — which is how a consumer feature ended up inside five
enterprise closes.**

Two audiences, two unrelated tests. The reader's test is whether the report tells them something about
themselves they could not already see, in language they can act on. The buyer's test is not a smaller
version of that one: they will likely never open a single report, and they are deciding whether to
renew a programme whose value is otherwise invisible to them. When the reader is the buyer, the useful
artifact and the sellable artifact are the same object and the second one comes free.

Here they come apart.

The commercial end of this is in my record — 5+ enterprise clients. The channel is not. Employer
health plans, insurers, hospitals, direct signup: that the reader and the payer are different parties
is an assumption the rest of this page rests on, and it is stated here as one.
[NEEDS: who pays, and how do readers arrive?]

<Drawer label="Why the buyer's evidence quietly starts setting the roadmap">

The buyer never opens a report. Their evidence is aggregate and second-hand: how many beneficiaries
received one, how many opened it, and what the programme sounds like described in a line by somebody
who has also never read one. So the report acquires a second output nobody sets out to design — its
own summary statistics — and the renewal decision gets made on those.

Which means you can build a report that summarises beautifully and helps nobody, and the loop will not
correct you. The buyer's signal arrives on the renewal cycle. The reader's signal does not arrive at
all unless somebody collects it.

The reader's usefulness is the leading indicator and the buyer's legibility is the lagging one. Decide
which one you optimise on the day they disagree, before they disagree, because lagging indicators are
the ones that pay you.

</Drawer>

## What a report should say is decided by what the reader can do next

Personalisation logic sounds like a ranking problem. **It is a series of decisions about what to say to
a person who did not ask you a question.**

Every one of those decisions needs the same input: what this reader is in a position to do about it. A
result nobody can act on is anxiety with a timestamp. Health results arrive in units most people
cannot interpret, and the distance between holding a number and knowing what it means about you is the
reason an artifact like this exists.

What I will not do is reconstruct the research. My record carries the scope of this work and not its
method — no interview count, no sample, no instrument — and to this audience a case study that invents
those reads worse than one admitting it does not have them.

One unanswered question decides most of what follows. Personalisation over self-reported data is a
different product from personalisation over lab results, and different again over wearable data,
because the second carries an interpretation burden the first does not.

## Where a commercial ask can sit inside a health report

The same artifact carries an interpretation of somebody's health and an ask. That is a real product
problem with no comfortable answer, because **the placement that converts best is the placement that
costs the most trust, and trust is the whole asset.** A report nobody believes is worth nothing to the
reader, and one renewal later it is worth nothing to the buyer either.

Reversibility decides the argument here, and it is the column most people leave out. Pulling a section
takes a deploy. Pulling a reader's conclusion that a health report is a sales surface takes
considerably longer, and you do not find out you need to until the number that would have told you has
already moved.

Which of the three shipped is not in my record, and I am not filling it in from memory on a page whose
whole argument is that I state things carefully.
[NEEDS: which placement shipped, and what did you rule out?]

<Callout variant="tradeoff" label="The decision in my record is the prior one">

What my record does say is that I created the hooks at all: this report would ask the reader for
something. The version that asks for nothing is easier to trust and easier to approve, and it earns
nothing, which inside a programme that has to be renewed is not a neutral outcome.

So the most commercially legible part of the product is also its most trust-expensive part. That is
the shape of the problem rather than an accident of this project. Anything that converts inside a
health artifact converts because the reader is in a receptive state, and the reason they are receptive
is that they were reading something they believed.

</Callout>

## Two outcomes, and both of them measure the buyer

What shipped: the reports, 0→1, with the requirements, the flow and the personalisation logic behind
them, and the cross-sell hooks inside them. No screenshots — the product is not mine to publish.

My record attributes 15% incremental revenue to the cross-sell hooks. Not to the reports, and I hold
that boundary because it is the one a sceptic draws for me in the first minute anyway. The reports
were separately cited as a key USP in closing 5+ enterprise clients. **I built the artifact; I did not
own the sale.** A deal closes for a dozen reasons, and a differentiator named in a close is not a
differentiator that caused it.

I attribute both directionally, not causally.

<Drawer label="15% incremental revenue is the weakest number in my record">

It has no baseline, no population, no period and no attribution method. Every other number I publish
has at least two of the four.

The baseline would have to be the revenue line the hooks sit inside as it stood before the reports
existed, not total revenue and not a line that moved for another reason in the same window. The
population would be whoever was eligible to see a hook, which is not everyone with an account and
probably not everyone who received a report.

The method is the one that changes the claim. Measured at the hook, as an attributable path from
report to purchase, it is a statement about my product. Inferred from a period-over-period movement,
it is a statement about a quarter that happened to contain my product.

Until I can say which, this is a direction and not a measurement, and it belongs on the page as one.

</Drawer>

<Callout variant="mistake" label="The read I never built">

**Everything that counted here counted on the buyer's side**, and both of those numbers are
contaminated: revenue moves with pricing and with a sales motion I did not run, and closes move with
everything.

The reader's read would have been cheap. Whether they take the specific next step their own report
recommends, broken out by what the report said; and whether they can restate what it told them a week
later. The first is an action rate you can instrument in a sprint. Neither needs randomisation,
because the question is not whether the report improved anyone's health — it is whether the report was
understood and acted on.

The commercial read arrives whether or not you build it. The reader's read only exists if somebody
builds it. So the default outcome of an artifact with a split audience is that you learn a great deal
about the buyer and very little about the person it was written for, and the shape of my record is
exactly that. Structural, not an oversight, which is the reason to design against it at the start.

</Callout>

## The first question an AI-PM interviewer asks about this is not in my record

Ask anyone who has shipped generative features in a regulated domain about a report like this and the
first question is always the same: how does it avoid giving medical advice? It is the right question,
and it is not a compliance question. A sentence that explains a result and a sentence that prescribes
a response to it are grammatically almost identical, and a model that is good at the first is good at
the second by accident.

**I am not going to answer that with an architecture I cannot show you.**

Where that line sat is the single thing an AI-PM reader most wants from this page.
[NEEDS: prompt, rules, templates, review, or none of them?]

If the answer is any of the first four, this is the strongest case study on the site and it belongs at
the front of it. Until that answer arrives it sits third, and says why.

<Drawer label="Four places the line between explaining and advising can live, and what each one costs">

This is argument, not an account of what was built.

**In the prompt** is the cheapest to write and the least testable. It fails silently, it fails
differently after a model update nobody scheduled, and you hear about it from a user instead of from a
test.

**In deterministic rules around the model** is testable, versionable, and fails loudly, which is the
property you want. It covers only what somebody sat down and enumerated, so its coverage is a document
and not a guarantee.

**In fixed templates the model fills** is the strongest guarantee available and the weakest product.
Past a certain amount of template you have decided not to generate, which should be a decision rather
than a discovery.

**In human review** is the only one that catches what nobody anticipated, and the only one whose cost
scales with volume instead of build time. A real answer at a thousand reports; not an answer at a
million.

Most real systems are a mix, and which mix you chose is the interesting half of the conversation.

</Drawer>

What does exist is a tool. [Grounded](/lab/grounded) scores generated health text on four things:
whether every claim traces to the input it was given, whether it steps outside its scope, whether it
hands off when it should, and whether an ordinary reader can follow it. I built it for this portfolio
and not at work, and it exists because the question above deserves a real answer and this case study
does not contain one.
