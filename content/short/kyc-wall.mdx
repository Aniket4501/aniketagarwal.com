Five steps of identity verification stood between a new user and the product,
and the funnel said that is where people stopped. That finding offers two very
different remedies. Make the five steps less painful, or stop making them a
precondition for seeing anything at all.

I proposed the second one, and took it to the CEO rather than treating it as a
flow change, because moving a compliance step is not a question about screens.
It is a question about what risk the company is willing to hold on its own
balance sheet for a while.

**Signup completion doubled, which is the least interesting sentence in this
case.** What doubling produced was a queue: accounts sitting inside the product
that had not been verified, each one a promise to finish something later. A
deferred step is a debt, and debts are only good decisions when there is a plan
for collection.

That plan was the nudge system — push and in-app, aimed at post-signup KYC
completion, shipped with engineering in three weeks. I would argue it is the
actual product here rather than a follow-up to it. Deferral moves the wall. Only
the nudges take it down.

The number I would want, and the one this record does not contain, is what
happened on the other side of the deferral.

"+100% signup completion" measures how many people got through a door, not how
many ended up in the room. A funnel that counts arrivals and never checks the
destination is the easiest kind to move and the least useful kind to report — so
if I ran this again, the recovery target would be set before the deferral
shipped, not after.
