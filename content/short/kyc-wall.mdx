Five steps of identity verification stood between a new user and the product,
and the funnel said that is where people stopped. That finding offers two very
different remedies. Make the five steps less painful, or stop making them a
precondition for seeing anything at all.

I proposed the second one, and took it to the CEO rather than handling it as a
flow change. Moving a compliance step is a decision about what risk the company
will hold and for how long, which makes it a decision above the level of anyone
redrawing a signup screen.

**Signup completion doubled, which is the least interesting sentence in this
case.** What doubling produced was a queue: accounts sitting inside the product
that had not been verified, each one a promise to finish something later. A
deferred step is a debt, and a debt is only a good decision when somebody has
written down how it gets collected.

Collection was the nudge system. Push and in-app, aimed at post-signup KYC
completion, shipped with engineering in three weeks — and I would argue it is
the product here, not the follow-up to it.

Deferral moves the wall. The nudges are what take it down.
