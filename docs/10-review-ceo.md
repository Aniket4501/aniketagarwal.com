# Agent 10 — Pass A — Startup CEO (Seed–Series B), 90 seconds, mobile only

Read: `docs/screenshots/iteration-2/home-390.png`, `docs/screenshots/iteration-2/home-390-full.png`. Nothing else.
Posture: I have no product org. I need someone who finds the problem, decides, ships, and tells me the truth after.

---

## 1. What I remember 90 seconds later

**"The app took fifteen seconds to open. I spent eight weeks there instead. We shipped it under two."**

That is the one thing, and it survives. It is a whole person in eighteen words: he looked at the roadmap he was handed, decided it was measuring the wrong thing, spent two months on the unglamorous fix, and it worked. `home-390.png` puts it in the first screen at display-size type with nothing competing. No "passionate about", no framework name, no photo. I could repeat it to a co-founder from memory.

The number that sticks with it is **15s → under 2s**, not 1M users. That is the right number to have stick.

## 2. Would I reply?

**YES.** Same day, short reply, "what did you cut to get eight weeks?"

Reason: the hero proves the only thing I actually screen for at this stage — that he will override a roadmap on evidence and then own the consequence. Most PM portfolios I open lead with scope ("owned the 0→1 roadmap for a 1M+ user platform"). This one leads with a decision that cost something. `home-390.png` is doing more work than most candidates' entire site.

The YES is earned in the first screen. Everything below it makes the yes slower, not stronger — see §3.

## 3. Strongest / weakest

**Strongest: the hero, `home-390.png`.** Headline, one-line consequence, five-line context paragraph, done. Nothing in the top screen is a claim about him; everything is a claim about the product. That is the correct instinct and it is rare.

**Weakest: the amber chip in the hero — `home-390.png`, the line reading `Currently: one clause — what is on your desk this month? · Open to PM and APM roles…`.**

This is a real defect and it is the highest-leverage fix on the page. The amber convention is defensible everywhere else — on the metric tiles ("which percentile, on which device population?" in `home-390-full.png`) it reads as a man who knows what a denominator is, and that is a point in his favour. But in the hero, on a 390px screen, *the fourth thing I see* is red dotted-underline placeholder text where his own status line should be. Everywhere else the chip says "I refuse to invent a number about the product." Here it says "I have not written my own headline yet." Those are opposite signals and the visual treatment is identical, so the reader has to work out which one it is — in the exact two seconds where I am deciding whether this is a finished site or a template someone abandoned. Nobody needs a denominator for "what is on your desk this month." That is not missing source data, it is an unwritten sentence. Cut it; the sentence works as `Currently: open to PM and APM roles in consumer product, health and applied AI.`

**Runner-up weakness — visible duplication in the metric strip.** In `home-390-full.png` the tile `BUNDLE SIZE / 25MB ▸ 6MB / 76% smaller` renders **twice within one and a half screens** (once footnoted "Two stated totals; the basis is the open question below", once footnoted "download, install, or APK/AAB size?"). Same number, same bar, two different questions. Then COLD START and SESSION TIME each appear a second time further down, attached to the case cards. Three numbers, six tiles. On a phone that is not a system, that is a loop that did not terminate. It also cheapens the numbers: repeating a stat is what people do when they only have one.

**Third: the ending.** `home-390-full.png` ends with two contact blocks stacked directly on top of each other — "If you want to argue with a number on this page…" and "If any of this is a problem you have right now…" — each with the same `aniketagarwalmhq24@gmail.com` and its own `copy` button, and the same address again in the hero. Three copies of one email address. Both lines are good lines. Pick one. The stack reads as a merge that nobody re-read.

## 4. The million users

Within five minutes of any conversation I will work out that the 1M+ came from an enterprise distribution channel he did not build — HCL sells a corporate health programme, employers hand over their headcount, and the app inherits the population. Registration was not his growth loop.

**Does the page get there before I do? Half.**

It gets there in substance, once, in `home-390-full.png` at card 03:

> "The beneficiary reads the report. The employer buys the programme."
> "A consumer feature that ended up inside the enterprise sale."

That is exactly right and it is the smartest line on the page — he understands the two-sided structure he was operating inside, and he understands that the person who reads the output is not the person who pays. And the track record entry later says "1M+ registered **beneficiaries**", which is the honest noun.

But the hero — the only part I am guaranteed to read — says "a consumer health app with **1M+ registered users**", and "users" in a hero is a growth claim. The correction sits roughly four screens down in a case-study headline, and the honest noun sits about nine screens down in the CV. So the page *volunteers* the truth but files it behind the flattering version. That is the one place where the site's own stated ethic — every number carries its population — is not applied to its biggest number.

Fix, and it is one clause: **"1M+ registered beneficiaries — an employer-funded population, not a growth number I built."** Saying it in the hero converts my "let me check that" into "he already checked it", and it costs him nothing, because his actual claim was never acquisition. He is not selling me growth. He is selling me that he made an inherited million-user surface usable. That is a *better* story than the one the hero currently implies, and the hero is currently underselling him to protect a number that will not survive the first phone call.

## 5. What would make me reply faster

1. **Put the beneficiary correction in the hero.** One clause, §4. Removes the only thing on the page I would have to fact-check.
2. **Delete the `[one clause — what is on your desk this month?]` chip from the hero.** It is the single worst-placed pixel on the site. Keep every other amber chip; they read as rigour once I have context. This one arrives before I have context.
3. **Deduplicate the metric strip.** One tile per number in `home-390-full.png`, three total, and let the case cards carry their own. Repetition makes three numbers feel like one.
4. **One contact block, not two-plus-one.** "If any of this is a problem you have right now, I'd like to hear about it" is the better of the two lines and is the correct closing sentence for my kind of reader. Delete the other.
5. **Cut the page roughly in half.** `home-390-full.png` is 14,838px tall — about 38 phone screens. I stopped being persuaded at screen four and everything after was tax. The hero, three cards, the Grounded demo, one contact block. The track record can live on /about; I will click if I care, and by then I already do.

**Net:** yes, I would take the call. He wrote one sentence that told me who he is, which almost nobody does. The failures here are all of the *finishing* kind — a placeholder left in the shop window, a stat printed twice, a CTA printed twice, a page that keeps going after it has won. None of them are judgement failures, which is why the answer stays yes. But on a phone, in ninety seconds, an unfinished-looking hero is the difference between a same-day reply and a starred tab I never come back to.
